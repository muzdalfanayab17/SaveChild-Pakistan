Child Safety

Introduction

What is Child Safety?

Child safety means protecting children from harm, abuse, neglect, bullying, and unsafe situations. It also means creating an environment where children can grow, learn, and express themselves safely.

Why Do Children Need a Safe Environment?

Children need a safe environment to support healthy development, build trust, learn confidently, and feel comfortable asking for help when something goes wrong.

Basic Types of Safety
  • Personal Safety: Understanding personal boundaries and recognizing situations that may be unsafe.
  • Physical Safety: Staying safe at home, school, on roads, and around potentially dangerous objects or situations.
  • Online Safety: Protecting personal information and staying safe while using websites, apps, games, and social media.
What Does SafeSteps Teach Children?
  • Awareness: Recognizing potentially unsafe situations.
  • Confidence: Speaking up when something feels wrong or uncomfortable.
  • Action: Knowing when to move away, seek help, and tell a trusted adult.

Personal Safety

Personal safety means understanding how to protect yourself and recognizing situations that may make you feel unsafe.

Basic Personal Safety Tips

  • Stay aware of your surroundings.
  • Stay close to trusted adults when necessary.
  • Speak up if something feels wrong.
  • Ask for help when you need it.
  • Know which adults you can trust.

Body Safety & Boundaries

Every child has the right to feel safe and respected. Understanding personal boundaries helps children recognize uncomfortable situations and know when to ask for help.

Understanding Personal Boundaries

  • Everyone has the right to personal boundaries.
  • You can say "No" when something makes you uncomfortable.
  • Private body parts should remain private.
  • Tell a trusted adult about an unsafe or uncomfortable situation.
  • Never feel that you have to keep a harmful secret.

Online Safety

The internet can be useful and fun, but children should know how to protect themselves while using websites, apps, games, and social media.

Stay Safe Online

  • Do not share personal information with strangers online.
  • Use strong and private passwords.
  • Be careful with unknown links and messages.
  • Use privacy settings where appropriate.
  • Tell a trusted adult if something online feels unsafe.

Bullying & Cyberbullying

Bullying can happen in person or online. It can include repeated hurtful behavior, threats, humiliation, or exclusion. Children should know that asking for help is not something to be ashamed of.

What Can You Do?

  • Tell a trusted adult.
  • Move away from an unsafe situation when possible.
  • Do not respond with violence.
  • Keep relevant messages or evidence when appropriate.
  • Ask for support if the problem continues.

Safe & Unsafe Situations

Safe Situations

  • Being with a trusted adult.
  • Asking a teacher for help.
  • Talking to parents about a problem.
  • Using the internet with safe practices.

Unsafe Situations

  • Someone threatening or hurting you.
  • Unwanted or uncomfortable contact.
  • A stranger asking for private information.
  • Threatening or inappropriate online messages.

What To Do If You Feel Unsafe

  1. Stay calm and move to a safer place if possible.
  2. Tell a trusted adult what happened.
  3. Ask for help and support.
  4. If there is immediate danger, seek appropriate emergency help.
  5. Do not keep serious safety concerns secret.

Trusted Adults

A trusted adult is a responsible person you feel comfortable talking to about a problem or safety concern.

  • Parent or guardian
  • Teacher
  • School counselor
  • Another responsible family member
  • Another trusted adult

If one person does not listen, keep asking another trusted adult for help.

Everyday Safety Tips

  • Stay aware of your surroundings.
  • Protect your personal information.
  • Speak up when something feels wrong.
  • Know who your trusted adults are.
  • Think carefully before sharing information online.
  • Ask for help when you need it.
